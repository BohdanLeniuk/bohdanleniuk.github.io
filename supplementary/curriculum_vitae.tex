	\section{Working Papers}

	\begin{itemize}
		\item[1.] ``Promotions, Unemployment, and Labor-Market Sorting Under Asymmetric Information,'' 
		\begin{itemize} \footnotesize 
			\item[] This paper studies how promotions and unemployment shape labor-market sorting and wages. 
              Using the 1979 National Longitudinal Survey of Youth (NLSY79), I show that promotions amplify 
              the wage losses associated with unemployment spells: previously promoted workers experience 
              larger wage losses upon re-employment, and those losses increase as the spell prolongs, even 
              conditional on pre-displacement wage and tenure. I interpret these facts through a structural 
              model of promotions and job search in which firms learn about worker ability from a worker's 
              history of promotions and unemployment spells. When hiring a worker out of unemployment, firms 
              use this information to assign him a position. Because unemployment duration is a noisy signal 
              of ability, the model implies that unemployment can serve as a shock to labor-market sorting 
              through which firms may either correct an erroneous promotion or incorrectly move capable 
              workers back down the job-ladder. Because UI benefits alter search behavior, they also change 
              how much an unemployment spell reveals about a worker. Relative to a flat benefit profile, UI 
              plans that tie benefits to past wages worsen sorting and lower aggregate output, while policies 
              that target benefits toward low-ability workers improve sorting and raise output.
		\end{itemize}
	\end{itemize}
